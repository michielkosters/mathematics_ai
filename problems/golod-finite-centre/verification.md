# Verification of Kourovka 21.132

The [complete proof](README.md) is an existence proof using Golod's theorem and a maximal homogeneous ideal. No finite computation or executable certificate verifies this infinite construction.

Check these implications in order:

1. Golod's homogeneous construction supplies an infinite-dimensional graded nil algebra over F_p, generated by three degree-one elements. See Examples 1–2, pp. 274–275 of [Golod (1964)](https://www.mathnet.ru/im2956).
2. The chain-union argument in the proof permits a maximal homogeneous ideal retaining infinite dimension. Every nonzero homogeneous ideal of the resulting algebra B therefore has finite codimension.
3. A nonzero homogeneous central element would generate a nilpotent ideal J of finite codimension. Then B^m is contained in J and J^k=0, forcing B^(mk)=0 and contradicting infinite dimension. Taking homogeneous components proves Z(B)=0.
4. The group H generated by the three elements 1+x_i is infinite, because its linear span is the entire unitization of B. Nilness makes every element have p-power order. Degree truncations separate elements in finite p-groups. Commutation with the generators proves Z(H)=1.
5. G=H times C_p consequently has all the required properties and centre exactly C_p. Adding a square-zero annihilating algebra generator t realizes this same group inside the adjoint group of B direct-sum F_p t. Four group generators suffice.

**Scope:** every prime p; an infinite finitely generated residually finite torsion p-group; centre of order p. This does not provide an algorithm for a computable presentation. No new Golod theorem or priority for this consequence is claimed. The independent AI audit reported no substantive logical gap; it is not formal verification or external peer review.
