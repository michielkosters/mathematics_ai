# A Golod construction with centre of order p

**Result.** For every prime $p$ there is an infinite finitely generated residually finite torsion $p$-group $G$ with

$$Z(G)\cong C_p.$$

The construction answers the properties requested in **A. V. Timofeenko's Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.132** (UnsolvedMath 2641). It remains within the adjoint-group construction from a finitely generated graded nil algebra over $\mathbb F_p$.

**Attribution and scope.** This is a consequence of Golod's classical nil-algebra construction and elementary algebra. No new version of the Golod–Shafarevich theorem, and no priority for the consequence, is claimed. The construction uses a maximal homogeneous ideal; it is an existence proof, not an algorithm for computing a presentation. The original statement imposes no indecomposability or fixed generator count.

## The short idea

Make a centreless Golod group $H$, then take $G=H\times C_p$. The direct product preserves finite generation, residual finiteness and the torsion $p$-property, and its centre is exactly $C_p$.

The only point requiring care is to obtain $H$ while preserving residual finiteness. The following graded-algebra argument does that.

## Complete proof

All algebras below are associative and have no identity. A *nil algebra* means each of its elements is nilpotent; the whole algebra need not be nilpotent.

**1. Start with Golod's algebra.** By Golod's construction, choose an infinite-dimensional nil algebra

$$A=\bigoplus_{n\geq1}A_n$$

over $\mathbb F_p$, generated by finitely many degree-one elements $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ (one may take $d=3$). Each $A_n$ is finite-dimensional. Here we use the homogeneous-ideal version of Golod's construction.

**2. Take a maximal homogeneous quotient that is still infinite-dimensional.** There exists a homogeneous ideal $I$ maximal subject to $\dim(A/I)=\infty$.

Here is the Zorn-lemma check. The union $U$ of a chain of eligible ideals is homogeneous. If $A/U$ were finite-dimensional, it would vanish in all sufficiently high degrees. In particular $A_N\subseteq U$ for some $N$. A finite basis of $A_N$ would then lie in a single ideal in the chain. Since $A$ is generated in degree one, that ideal contains every $A_n$ for $n\geq N$, contradicting its infinite codimension.

Put $B=A/I$. Every nonzero homogeneous ideal of $B$ has finite codimension, by maximality. The algebra $B$ is still graded, nil, generated in degree one, and infinite-dimensional.

**3. The algebra $B$ has zero centre.** Suppose $b$ is a nonzero homogeneous central element. Choose $k$ with $b^k=0$. In the unitization $B^+=\mathbb F_p1\oplus B$, its generated ideal is

$$J=B^+b,\qquad J^k=0,$$

because $b$ is central. This is a nonzero homogeneous ideal of $B$, so $B/J$ is finite-dimensional. Consequently all sufficiently high homogeneous components of $B$ lie in $J$, and $B^m\subseteq J$ for some $m$. Then $B^{mk}=0$. A finitely generated nilpotent algebra is finite-dimensional, a contradiction.

Finally, every homogeneous component of a central element is central: commute it with each degree-one generator and compare degrees. Thus $Z(B)=0$.

**4. Pass to the group.** Write $\bar x_i$ for the images in $B$, and set

$$H=\langle1+\bar x_1,\ldots,1+\bar x_d\rangle\leq1+B.$$

The set $1+B$ is a group, since the inverse of $1+b$ is the finite geometric series in $-b$.

- **Infinite:** the linear span of $H$ in $B^+$ is a subalgebra containing $1$ and all $\bar x_i$, hence it is all of $B^+$. If $H$ were finite, $B$ would be finite-dimensional.
- **Finitely generated:** this is its definition.
- **Torsion $p$-group:** for each $b\in B$, choose $e$ with $b^{p^e}=0$. Then $(1+b)^{p^e}=1$.
- **Residually finite:** the maps to $1+(B/B_{\geq n})$, where $B_{\geq n}=\bigoplus_{j\geq n}B_j$, have finite targets and separate elements. In fact these targets are finite $p$-groups.
- **Centreless:** if $1+b$ commutes with the generators $1+\bar x_i$, then $b$ commutes with every $\bar x_i$, hence with all of $B$. Step 3 gives $b=0$.

**5. Add the required centre.** Let $G=H\times C_p$. It is infinite, finitely generated, residually finite, and a torsion $p$-group, with

$$Z(G)=Z(H)\times C_p=C_p.$$

It also remains a Golod-type adjoint construction. Set $D=B\oplus\mathbb F_pt$ with $t^2=tB=Bt=0$, and give $t$ degree one. This is a finitely generated graded nil algebra of infinite dimension. In $1+D$, the subgroup generated by $1+\bar x_i$ and $1+t$ is precisely $H\times C_p$. Thus four generators suffice. This completes the construction. $\square$

## Verification

See the [short verification guide](verification.md).

This is an infinite-group existence proof; finite Sage computations would not certify its conclusion. The checks are the five steps above. In particular, residual finiteness is proved using homogeneous degree quotients rather than assumed to survive an arbitrary quotient.
